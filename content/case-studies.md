## 1. Revolution Robotics

**Title:** Building robots is complex. Learning to build them shouldn't be.
Redesigning the robotics experience to help kids confidently build, code and
learn on their own.

**Context**
Revolution Robotics Foundation is a non-profit on a mission to make robotics
education affordable, accessible, and fun for kids aged 8–14. Their mobile
app — the primary tool guiding young builders through construction and code
— had gone stale, and was creating more friction than it removed. They
brought me on as UX Lead, working alongside a UX researcher over seven
months, to rebuild it.

**Role**
UX Lead, Product Designer. I led client communication, ran discovery
workshops, built interactive prototypes, and delivered the final UI.

**Process**
We started with discovery workshops to understand the foundation's mission
and, more importantly, how kids actually experience the app — not how adults
assume they do. Usability testing with 8–14 year-olds surfaced the real
friction points: too much dependence on adult help, and an information
architecture that made sense to us, not to them. Every decision after that
was tested against real kids, not our own instincts about what
"kid-friendly" should look like.

**Outcome**
A restructured information architecture, a refreshed UI and brand direction,
and a design system built to scale with the foundation's future. We tested
with 45 kids in total — validating a product that's not just intuitive, but
genuinely empowering: kids building and programming robots on their own, no
adult required.

## 3. Beacon (Orchestro)

**Title:** Ideas don't become viable ventures by default. Designing how
agentic AI helps founders research, validate and turn ideas into ventures.

> Source: live page (adamweberdesign.studio/orchestro) is still
> password-protected, so this is built from the public facts (McKinsey &
> Company, 2024–now, Creative Direction / Design System / Product Designer
> / Design Lead) plus the outcomes brief you provided directly. No metrics
> or specifics beyond what's in that brief are included.

**Context**
When I joined, design didn't yet have an established role in how the
product actually got built. Beacon (part of the Orchestro Suite at
McKinsey & Company) needed more than a designer executing screens — it
needed someone to define what design's role in the build process even was,
in a fast-moving, engineering-led B2B environment.

**Role**
Design Lead, Creative Direction, Design System, Product Designer. McKinsey
& Company, 2024–now.

**Process**
I started by defining a clear design vision and set of principles to build
from, then used that foundation to grow a scalable, robust design system
that unified the product experience. Rather than stay downstream of
engineering, I introduced collaborative design reviews and embedded design
earlier in the development process — showing, not just arguing, how design
could drive clarity, efficiency and innovation from the start.

That shift compounded: the design team grew, the org became more
design-aware, and partnerships with product, engineering and leadership got
noticeably closer. I also made direct UX research a standing priority, even
though B2B environments make user access harder to come by — talking to
users directly and feeding their input straight into decisions, rather than
relying on secondhand assumptions.

I'm currently leading the redesign of the platform into a chat-led
experience, using working prototypes built in Cursor to test new feature
ideas faster and more iteratively than static mockups would allow.

**Outcome**
A scalable design system that unified the product experience, a
development process where design now shapes decisions early instead of
being bolted on late, and a design-aware culture with real partnership
across product, engineering and leadership. The platform's chat-led redesign
is in progress, validated through rapid, AI-assisted prototyping rather than
guesswork.
